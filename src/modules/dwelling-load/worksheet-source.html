Dwelling service load

Collect the nameplates. Review the assumptions. See the calculation.

01 / Project & code basis

A calculation aid for a qualified electrical professional, not approval of an installation. Final service adequacy also depends on verified equipment, conductors, service ratings, phase loading and AHJ requirements. No generator sizing is performed.
The basis text is a project record, not a formula selector. This version uses 2017-style rules only. For NC one-/two-family dwellings, OSFM identifies the 2017 NEC plus NC amendments; confirm applicability before use.

02 / Equipment inventory

Enter nameplate VA per unit, not breaker size. Convert W only where power factor treatment is justified. Add every load once. Fixed-appliance rows must qualify for 220.53: no ranges, dryers, space heating or air conditioning. Standalone motor rows are counted at full load; do not repeat motors already inside another appliance or HVAC row.

HVAC rows are documentation only; their applied demand is entered below. “Additional reviewed load” is already-calculated VA at 100% in both methods, outside the optional demand pool (e.g., a separately reviewed EV/continuous load). Do not use it as a shortcut for unknown rules.

03 / Cooking, dryers & motors

Unusual or mixed cooking equipment is deliberately not guessed. Review household vs commercial classification, ratings and applicable Table 220.55 notes. Optional calculations use cooking nameplates, not the standard table demand.

Standard method adds 25% of the applicable largest motor. Its 100% base must already be counted elsewhere. Check embedded HVAC/appliance motors, use the proper code motor-load basis, and avoid an adder already included in reviewed HVAC or other VA. Optional method does not add another automatic 25%.

04 / HVAC & optional eligibility

Multiple systems and electric backup heat need a simultaneous-operation review. Do not simply choose the larger of every heat/cool label: compressor plus supplemental heat can operate together. Document standard noncoincident treatment (220.60 / applicable motor rules) and optional 220.82(C) treatment separately.
